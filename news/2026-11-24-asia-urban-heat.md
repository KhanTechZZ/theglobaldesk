# South Asia's Growing Cities Face a Rising Heat Challenge

Cities across South Asia are centres of employment, education, transport and economic activity. But rapid urban growth, dense construction and rising temperatures are creating new challenges for the people who live and work in them.

Extreme heat is not only an environmental issue. It can reduce labour productivity, place pressure on health services, disrupt businesses and make outdoor work increasingly difficult.

A 2026 World Bank report on extreme heat in South Asian cities highlights the growing risks to jobs, livelihoods and economic development. It identifies heat-resilient infrastructure, early warning systems, sustainable cooling and stronger urban planning as important parts of the response.

The challenge is particularly significant for people whose work requires them to spend long hours outdoors, as well as households with limited access to cooling.

Urban trees, shaded public spaces, better building design and reliable public services can help cities become more comfortable and resilient.

South Asia's urban future will depend not only on how quickly its cities grow, but also on how effectively they protect the people who make those cities work.
