# Coastal Communities Must Be Part of Climate Adaptation

Coastal communities experience environmental change in practical ways. They depend on fisheries, local transport, agriculture, tourism and other activities connected to the sea.

Climate adaptation is often discussed through large infrastructure projects and national policies. These are important, but local knowledge and community participation also matter.

The United Nations Environment Programme describes ecosystem-based adaptation as an approach that uses nature and ecosystem services to help people adapt to climate impacts.

In coastal areas, this can include the protection and restoration of mangroves, coral reefs, seagrass beds, dunes and wetlands.

Such ecosystems can provide important benefits, including coastal protection and habitats for biodiversity. Their condition also affects the livelihoods of nearby communities.

However, conservation plans are more likely to be effective when the people who depend on these landscapes are included in decision-making.

Local participation should not be limited to consultation after a project has already been designed. Communities should have opportunities to contribute to planning, implementation and monitoring.

A resilient coastline is not built only with structures. It also depends on healthy ecosystems and the people who understand them.
