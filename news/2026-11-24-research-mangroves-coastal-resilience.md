# Mangrove Forests: A Natural Defence for Vulnerable Coastlines

Mangrove forests grow along tropical and subtropical coastlines, where salt-tolerant trees and shrubs survive in tidal environments.

These ecosystems perform several important functions. Their roots and vegetation can help reduce coastal erosion, provide habitats for wildlife and support fisheries. Mangroves also store carbon in their biomass and waterlogged soils.

The Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations identifies mangroves as important natural defences against coastal erosion and storm surges. Its work also highlights their contribution to livelihoods and biodiversity.

The Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change recognises that coastal ecosystems, including mangroves, salt marshes and seagrass beds, provide important ecosystem services such as coastal protection and habitat for diverse species.

Yet mangroves face pressure from coastal development, aquaculture, pollution and changing environmental conditions.

Protecting these forests requires more than planting trees. It involves conserving existing ecosystems, restoring damaged areas, supporting local communities and monitoring whether restoration efforts are successful.

For coastal regions, mangroves are not simply green landscapes. They are part of a wider natural system that supports people and helps reduce environmental risks.
