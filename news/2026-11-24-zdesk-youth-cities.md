# Young People Should Help Shape the Cities They Will Inherit

Young people are not only future residents of cities. They are students, workers, entrepreneurs, commuters and active members of neighbourhoods today.

Urban decisions about housing, transport, public spaces, employment and environmental protection directly influence their opportunities.

UN-Habitat has highlighted the importance of meaningful youth participation in sustainable urban development. Its work argues that young people should be recognised as partners in shaping urban futures, not simply as beneficiaries of development programmes.

Youth participation can take many forms. Students can contribute to neighbourhood research, young professionals can share technical expertise, and community groups can help identify problems in public spaces and local services.

However, participation must be meaningful. Inviting young people to meetings without giving their contributions a place in decision-making risks turning engagement into a symbolic exercise.

Cities benefit when different generations can contribute to planning and public policy.

The future of a city is not determined only by its buildings and roads. It is also shaped by who gets to participate in decisions about how the city grows.
