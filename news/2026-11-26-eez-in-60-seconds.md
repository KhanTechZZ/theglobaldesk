An exclusive economic zone (EEZ) is a sea area beyond a country's territorial sea. Under the UN Convention on the Law of the Sea, it can extend up to **200 nautical miles (about 370 km)** from the coastal baselines.

In that zone, the coastal state has special rights to explore, use, conserve and manage natural resources in the water, seabed and subsoil. It also has jurisdiction over marine scientific research and protection of the marine environment.

An EEZ is not the same as owning all the water. Other countries keep important freedoms, including navigation and overflight, under international law.

*Source: [UN Convention on the Law of the Sea, Part V](https://www.un.org/Depts/los/convention_agreements/texts/unclos/part5.htm).*
